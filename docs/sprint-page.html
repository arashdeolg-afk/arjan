01Why this and nothing else

Every other option fails the 7-day test for a structural reason.

OptionWhy it can't pay inside 7 days
Ad revenueThreshold-gated. 1,000 subs / 10k followers minimum. Not reachable.
AffiliateNeeds traffic volume you don't have. Realistically $0–$20.
Digital productsNeeds an audience to sell to. You're building one, not done.
MerchNeeds a recurring character people already love.
A viral hitNot a plan. A lottery ticket with someone else's algorithm.
Client workVolume you control. 15 contacts → 1 sale, so 45 → ~3.
There is no algorithm in the loop. No audience requirement, no threshold to wait for. You already own the production capability — this points it at someone holding a budget.

02The offer

A free 5-second animated spot for their product, made before they reply.
If they want a longer one: $200, 48-hour turnaround.

This converts where a normal pitch dies, for three reasons:

MechanismWhy it works
Removes imaginationThey aren't evaluating a promise. They're watching their own product already animated.
ReciprocityYou gave first, no strings. Rare enough in a DM inbox to earn a reply on its own.
Filters instantlySomeone who watches their product animated and feels nothing was never going to buy. You lose them in a day, not a month.

Cost to you: about $0.50–$2.00 in credits and 10 minutes per spec piece. Forty across the week is $40–80.

03Who to target

Aim precisely. The aesthetic has to match or the spec piece lands wrong.

SegmentWhyWhere
Indie game devs
best target		 Launching in <60 days. Deadline, allocated budget, no in-house animator. Steam Coming Soon · itch.io · r/IndieDev · #screenshotsaturday
Anime & gaming Shopify stores Exact aesthetic match. Buy creative continuously, have revenue to pay from. Anime apparel / figures / peripherals · IG brands running static ads

The one filter that matters: a dated launch inside 60 days. It means budget is already allocated and the deadline is real. A dev with no date has no reason to decide this week.

Do not pitch anime-style work to plumbing companies or law firms. The style mismatch kills a good offer.

LineWhy it's there
The specific detailThe whole message. Proves this isn't a blast. Can't find one in 30 seconds? Skip the prospect.
"Free, no catch"Pre-empts the suspicion that kills most cold DMs.
Price statedNo "let's hop on a call." Removing the negotiation step is what makes a 48-hour close possible.
Offer as a P.S.The gift has to read as a gift, not as bait.

06Why $200 is the right number

Market rate for indie animation runs about $39/hour, and a traditional animator quotes $200–$800 for a 15–30 second spot.

WhenPriceWhy
First 3 clients$150–200You're buying testimonials, not maximising rate
After 3 testimonials$400Proof lets you double
Rush (<24h)+50%Deadline pressure is worth real money to them
Price against their alternative, never against your cost. $200 sits at the bottom of the range they already expect. The piece takes you about an hour — roughly 5x market hourly. That gap is the whole business, and it exists because AI collapsed production time while market price hasn't moved yet.

Take payment on delivery under $300 — a deposit adds a step and costs more in lost deals than it saves in risk. Above $300, 50% upfront.

07Honest odds

Running the full 44 contacts.

~10replies
2–3closes at $200
75–85%chance of at least one sale
15–25%chance of zero
The zero case is real and usually means the targeting or spec quality was off — not that the model is wrong. The single biggest predictor is volume actually sent. Nearly everyone who fails at this fails by sending 8 messages instead of 44, then concluding it doesn't work.
